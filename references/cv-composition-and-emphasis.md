# CV Composition, Coverage, and Emphasis

Read this guide before building or reviewing a tailored CV. It prevents a targeted version from becoming a sparse keyword sheet, a wall of text, or a document that hides the candidate's broader professional value.

## Start with the full evidence library

Maintain a private master CV and a complete `EXPERIENCE_PROFILE.md`. They are the source of truth for all verified roles, projects, certifications, capabilities, and strong bullets. A tailored CV is a selected and reordered view of that library; it is never a replacement for it.

Make a brief tailoring plan before authoring:

| Evidence | Target relevance | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Direct role responsibility, project, or tool | Lead | Place early; use the strongest supported bullet first |
| Adjacent capability or transferable contribution | Support | Keep visible in the chronology, a bullet, project, or skill category |
| True but weakly connected detail | Reserve | Omit only when space or clarity requires it |

The plan must protect breadth. For a multidisciplinary candidate, a Product Designer version can lead with product thinking, research, flows, high-fidelity UI, and design systems while still retaining verified front-end implementation, visual identity, or other adjacent strengths. Targeting is prioritization, not erasure.

## Experience and project structure

Use a repeatable hierarchy for every included role:

```text
Role Title | Company                                  Month Year - Month Year
Location
• Action + specific context + verified outcome, scope, method, or deliverable
• Action + specific context + verified outcome, scope, method, or deliverable
• Action + specific context + verified outcome, scope, method, or deliverable
```

- Recent or material roles: two to four bullets.
- Older or supporting roles: one or two bullets.
- Selected projects: one or two bullets, ideally covering the problem, contribution, and verified output.
- Keep bullets as separate paragraphs, normally one to two rendered lines. Do not join distinct facts with semicolons into a dense paragraph.
- Start with a precise action verb. Favor delivered work, decisions, artifacts, methods, and outcomes over task labels.
- Use a metric only when the source supports it or the user confirms it. If no metric exists, show truth through scope, tool, methodology, deliverable, audience, or collaboration.

## Intentional emphasis

Bold is a scanning tool, not decoration. Use it to create a clear ladder:

1. Candidate name
2. Section headings
3. Role titles and, when useful, organizations
4. Skill-category labels
5. At most one short, truthful phrase in a bullet when that phrase is the key role signal

Do not bold every skill, every action verb, or whole bullet sentences. The reader should be able to scan the bold text and understand the professional story without feeling that the page is shouting.

## Page balance and density

One page does not need to be mechanically full, but it must look intentionally composed. An early-career candidate with several roles and projects should not have a large empty lower region while relevant verified content has been omitted.

When a one-page draft is visibly underfilled:

1. Restore the strongest omitted `Support` bullet for each meaningful role.
2. Add a relevant project, case study, certification, or objective skill category from the evidence library.
3. Expand an overly compressed role into distinct, evidence-supported bullets.
4. Reassess the summary; replace generic claims with a concrete supported professional proposition.
5. If evidence still does not warrant more content, keep the whitespace. Never invent, repeat, enlarge margins, or artificially spread lines just to fill the page.

When the draft is too dense, remove `Reserve` material before shrinking body type or margins. Use two pages when verified material is genuinely stronger than an unreadably compressed one-page result.

## ATS version versus designed version

For online portals and unknown parsing systems, create the ATS version: one column, selectable text, standard headings, clean bullets, and restrained typography.

For direct email, networking, portfolio downloads, or a creative employer that explicitly does not use ATS, offer a separate designed version only if the user wants it. It may express more personal visual identity, but it must remain readable and must not replace the ATS master. Keep its content aligned with the master so the candidate does not create conflicting career stories.

## Final visual gate

After rendering every page, confirm:

- the name, role title, headings, and role titles create a clear scan path;
- each experience and project reads as distinct bullet paragraphs, not a run-on block;
- bold emphasis is sparse and purposeful;
- dates and locations align consistently;
- no large unused page area remains while relevant verified evidence is available;
- the target role is prominent, but the candidate's material complementary strengths remain visible; and
- the layout is appropriate to the submission channel: ATS-safe for portals, designed only by explicit choice for direct creative use.
